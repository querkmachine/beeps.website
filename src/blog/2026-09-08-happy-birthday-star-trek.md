Today marks 60 years since the official premiere of _Star Trek_, the cult classic television programme about an, at the time, unusually diverse cast of folks exploring space in the not-so-distant future.

Since then, the franchise has seen an additional 11 television series, four miniseries, 14 movies, and an uncountable quantity of spin-off media. Something in the _Star Trek_ canon has been in production for virtually the entirety of those six decades.

I've banged on a lot about being a furry on this website. The furry fandom has easily been the most influential community in my personal and professional life, and I owe them a lot.

But it's also no competition that _Star Trek_ comes second. It's a franchise that I love, and though I only really interact with the peripheries of the fandom, I am very much a Trekkie.

I make a point of not being a toxic fan. I like _The Next Generation_ and I like _Discovery_, I like _Enterprise_ and I even like _Section 31_ to some small degree. "In the Pale Moonlight" is an amazing episode and "Sub Rosa" is... an interesting episode.

I very much subscribe to the idea of _Star Trek_ as a place, a phrase coined by [_The Greatest Generation_](https://greatesttrek.com/)'s Adam Pranica that establishes the _Star Trek_ universe as a setting where a sitcom, a courtroom drama, a western shoot-out, and a preschool cartoon can all coexist. The belief that the universe is large enough for all of them, and **not every version of _Trek_ has to be for you**.

Anyway, anyway, thank you _Star Trek_ for being so great at so much. I look forward to many more years of philosophising about the nature of being, solving differences with diplomacy and mutual understanding, and watching fictional characters be incredibly competent in the face of unearthly adversity.

Happy birthday.
