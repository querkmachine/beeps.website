The paperwork has been signed, the deposit has been paid, the contracts have been exchanged. Unless an act of god blows the building over or some part of the machinations of bureaucracy goes wrong, I'm going to be a homeowner.

...Of a house 250 miles away from where I currently live.

Ah.

So I'm moving to Manchester, England's definitive second city (suck it, Birmingham). For those not privy to the beeps lore, Manchester and its surrounds are where I lived and grew up before I moved to Bristol in 2010. My family live around there still, as do a lot of friends I'm still in varying levels of contact with.

If I can't live in Bristol, it seems like the second best option available.

## The money game

The main driver has really been because house prices in Bristol are kinda crazy bad value. By way of comparison:

|                                   |   Bristol | Greater Manchester |
| :-------------------------------- | --------: | -----------------: |
| Median annual salary              |  £ 40,210 |           £ 35,222 |
| Median house price                | £ 348,750 |          £ 240,000 |
| House price as multiple of salary |      8.67 |               6.81 |

A house in Bristol costs close to 9 times the average salary for the area, compared to less than 7 times the average in Manchester.

And that's not taking into account that the houses being sold in Bristol trend towards being smaller than those in Manchester. Break it down by property type and things look even worse.

| Median price per property type |   Bristol | Greater Manchester |
| :----------------------------- | --------: | -----------------: |
| Detached                       | £ 550,000 |          £ 390,000 |
| Semi-detached                  | £ 370,000 |          £ 260,000 |
| Terrace                        | £ 375,000 |          £ 195,000 |
| Flat                           | £ 258,000 |          £ 170,000 |

Paying the median house price in Bristol gets you a flat or, if you're lucky, a cheap terrace. The median house in Manchester gets you a nice terrace or a slightly cheap semi-detached place.

I've lived too much of my life in flats by now. I want a garden, damn it.

{% character character="ash", variant="thinking" %}
If you're curious why I'm comparing the entirity of Greater Manchester to just the City of Bristol (and not surrounding areas like South Gloucestershire and North Somerset), it's because I consider it a more apt comparison to make.

Virtually all of Greater Manchester is a single urban area with strong transport links, akin to Greater London. Bristol's surrounding counties include a lot of rural areas, and they're not as well connected to Bristol by public transit.
{% endcharacter %}

## Going, going...

I _really_ like Bristol. It's a city of great diversity and progressiveness that fuels an hyperactive arts and food scene. It's a city very unafraid of acknowledging its flaws, both past and present, of saying what it feels needs to be said, or doing what it feels needs to be done. And it's small enough that you can pretty much walk anywhere you need to go, too.

I'm going to miss this place a lot. I suppose that's to be expected when you've called a place home for nearly half of your life.
